5CTA0 · Statistical Signal Processing block 4

Which tail is the whole exam.

Estimation outputs a number. Detection outputs a label: \(\mathcal H_0\) or \(\mathcal H_1\). The two densities overlap, so every rule makes mistakes. The optimal rule is always a likelihood ratio test. Neyman–Pearson picks the threshold from \(P_{\mathrm{FA}}=\alpha\). Bayes picks it from costs and priors. After algebra you test a scalar. Which side of that scalar is \(\mathcal H_1\) is not always the right tail.

Exam block, every year: write \(L=p(\mathbf x\mid\mathcal H_1)/p(\mathbf x\mid\mathcal H_0)\), simplify to \(T\gtrless\lambda'\), re-derive the inequality, shade, then \(Q\) / erf / exponential CDF.

Four events

Model: \(\mathcal H_0:\ \mathbf x\sim p(\mathbf x\mid\mathcal H_0)\), \(\mathcal H_1:\ \mathbf x\sim p(\mathbf x\mid\mathcal H_1)\). A detector partitions observation space into \(\mathcal X_0\cup\mathcal X_1=\mathbb R^N\). If \(\mathbf x\in\mathcal X_i\), decide \(\mathcal H_i\).

true \(\mathcal H_0\)true \(\mathcal H_1\)
decide \(\mathcal H_0\)true negative (TN)miss / false negative / type II
decide \(\mathcal H_1\)false alarm / type Idetection / true positive

Row = decision, column = truth. Memorise by axes, not by the words “positive / negative”.

$$P_D=\int_{\mathcal X_1}p(\mathbf x\mid\mathcal H_1)\,d\mathbf x,\qquad P_{\mathrm{FA}}=\int_{\mathcal X_1}p(\mathbf x\mid\mathcal H_0)\,d\mathbf x,\qquad P_M=1-P_D.$$

If the test on a 1-D statistic is “decide \(\mathcal H_1\) when \(T>\lambda'\)” (usual right-tail mean-shift):

$$P_{\mathrm{FA}}=1-F_{T\mid\mathcal H_0}(\lambda'),\qquad P_D=1-F_{T\mid\mathcal H_1}(\lambda').$$

The inequality can flip. If \(\mathcal H_1\) is the narrower density (small variance, large exponential rate), large \(|x|\) favours \(\mathcal H_0\), and you decide \(\mathcal H_1\) when the statistic is small. Then \(P_{\mathrm{FA}}\) is a left tail of \(p(\,\cdot\mid\mathcal H_0)\). Re-derive \(\gtrless\); do not memorise “right tail”.

Shading, always:

  • \(P_{\mathrm{FA}}\) = \(\mathcal H_0\) density on the \(\mathcal H_1\)-decision side.
  • \(P_D\) = \(\mathcal H_1\) density on the \(\mathcal H_1\)-decision side.
  • \(P_M\) = \(\mathcal H_1\) density on the \(\mathcal H_0\)-decision side.

Reader Fig. 14.4 (mean-shift, decide \(\mathcal H_1\) if \(\bar x>\lambda'\)): left panel \(P_{\mathrm{FA}}\) shades the right tail of \(p(\bar x\mid\mathcal H_0)\) (centred at 0). Right panel \(P_D\) shades the right tail of \(p(\bar x\mid\mathcal H_1)\) (centred at \(A\)). The reader’s OCR caption on the left panel wrongly says “under \(\mathcal H_1\)”. Trust the figure, not the OCR.

You cannot move the threshold to improve both error types. Liberal (threshold toward the noise mean): more hits and more FAs. Conservative (threshold toward the signal mean): fewer FAs and more misses.

Likelihood ratio test

Regardless of NP or Bayes, the optimal test compares the likelihood ratio to a threshold:

$$\Lambda(\mathbf x)=L(\mathbf x)=\frac{p(\mathbf x\mid\mathcal H_1)}{p(\mathbf x\mid\mathcal H_0)}\;\underset{\mathcal H_0}{\overset{\mathcal H_1}{\gtrless}}\;\gamma.$$

Decide \(\mathcal H_1\) if \(L\ge\gamma\), else \(\mathcal H_0\). \(L\) is a scalar even if \(\mathbf x\in\mathbb R^N\). \(\ln L\) is monotone, so the log-LRT is equivalent for \(\gamma>0\). After simplification one compares a sufficient statistic \(T(\mathbf x)\) to a new threshold \(\lambda'\). That \(\lambda'\) is not \(\gamma\); it absorbs constants, \(\sigma^2\), \(N\), \(\ln\gamma\).

Some old keys write \(L=p(\mathcal H_0)/p(\mathcal H_1)\) (Jan 2019 Answers_oldexams). Use \(L=p_1/p_0\). The probability table in that key is the standard right-tail one; the inverted ratio is a typesetting bug, not a different test.

Neyman–Pearson vs Bayes

Neyman–Pearson

Maximise \(P_D\) subject to \(P_{\mathrm{FA}}\le\alpha\). The optimum is an LRT. Any other region with the same \(P_{\mathrm{FA}}\) has strictly smaller \(P_D\).

  1. Write \(L(\mathbf x)\) and simplify to \(T(\mathbf x)\gtrless\lambda'\). Watch the inequality direction.
  2. Under \(\mathcal H_0\), find the distribution of \(T\). Set \(\lambda'\) so that \(P(T\text{ on the }\mathcal H_1\text{ side}\mid\mathcal H_0)=\alpha\). For Gaussians this is a \(Q\)-function inversion. Only the \(\mathcal H_0\) law enters here.
  3. Under \(\mathcal H_1\), compute \(P_D=P(T\text{ on the }\mathcal H_1\text{ side}\mid\mathcal H_1)\).

Lowering \(\gamma\) enlarges \(\mathcal X_1\): both \(P_D\) and \(P_{\mathrm{FA}}\) increase.

Bayes

Priors \(P_0=P(\mathcal H_0)\), \(P_1=1-P_0\). Cost \(C_{i,j}=\) cost of deciding \(\mathcal H_i\) when \(\mathcal H_j\) is true. Minimising Bayes risk again yields an LRT, now with a known threshold

$$\gamma=\frac{(C_{1,0}-C_{0,0})P_0}{(C_{0,1}-C_{1,1})P_1}.$$

Usually \(C_{1,1}=C_{0,0}=0\). Then \(\gamma=C_{1,0}P_0/(C_{0,1}P_1)\). Minimum probability of error: \(C_{0,1}=C_{1,0}=1\), so \(\gamma=P_0/P_1\) and \(\mathcal R=P_0 P_{\mathrm{FA}}+P_1 P_M\). Equivalent MAP: pick the hypothesis with larger posterior.

NP vs Bayes \(\gamma\). NP: \(\lambda'\) from \(P_{\mathrm{FA}}=\alpha\) using only \(p(\,\cdot\mid\mathcal H_0)\). Bayes: \(\gamma\) from costs and priors, no \(\alpha\). “Bayesian detection maximises Bayes risk” is false (it minimises). Do not set \(\gamma=1\) unless the costs and priors actually give that.

ROC

Plot \(P_D\) vs \(P_{\mathrm{FA}}\) as \(\gamma\) runs from \(0\) (always say \(\mathcal H_1\): \(P_D=P_{\mathrm{FA}}=1\)) to \(\infty\) (always say \(\mathcal H_0\): both 0).

  • NP ROC is the upper boundary of the feasible \((P_{\mathrm{FA}},P_D)\) set. Concave, non-decreasing, through \((0,0)\) and \((1,1)\).
  • Slope at a point equals the LR threshold that realises that point: \(dP_D/dP_{\mathrm{FA}}=\gamma\).
  • Diagonal \(P_D=P_{\mathrm{FA}}\) = coin-flip / identical densities. Better detectors bow toward the top-left \((0,1)\).
  • If a test sits below the diagonal, swap \(\mathcal X_0\) and \(\mathcal X_1\) (flip the inequality). That is why exponential / Laplace problems say “design so that \(P_D>P_{\mathrm{FA}}\)”.

Four standard LRTs

IID samples. Always start from \(L=\prod_n p(x_n\mid\mathcal H_1)/p(x_n\mid\mathcal H_0)\), take \(\ln\), dump constants into \(\gamma\).

1. Mean-shift Gaussian — threshold on \(\bar x\)

$$\mathcal H_0:\ x_n\sim\mathcal N(0,\sigma^2),\qquad\mathcal H_1:\ x_n\sim\mathcal N(A,\sigma^2),\quad A>0.$$ $$\begin{aligned} L(\mathbf x) &=\exp\Bigl(-\frac1{2\sigma^2}\sum_n\bigl[(x_n-A)^2-x_n^2\bigr]\Bigr) =\exp\Bigl(-\frac1{2\sigma^2}(NA^2-2NA\bar x)\Bigr),\\ \bar x &\;\underset{\mathcal H_0}{\overset{\mathcal H_1}{\gtrless}}\; \frac{\sigma^2}{NA}\ln\gamma+\frac A2=\lambda'. \end{aligned}$$

Right-tail test on the sample mean. Under \(\mathcal H_0\), \(\bar x\sim\mathcal N(0,\sigma^2/N)\); under \(\mathcal H_1\), \(\bar x\sim\mathcal N(A,\sigma^2/N)\).

$$P_{\mathrm{FA}}=Q\Bigl(\frac{\lambda'}{\sigma/\sqrt N}\Bigr),\qquad P_D=Q\Bigl(\frac{\lambda'-A}{\sigma/\sqrt N}\Bigr).$$

NP: \(\lambda'=(\sigma/\sqrt N)\,Q^{-1}(\alpha)\). Same algebra as the matched filter when the “mean” is a known waveform \(s_n\) instead of a DC (see below).

2. Unequal-variance Gaussians — sum of squares

Nov 2019 Q4, official key.

$$\mathcal H_0:\ x[n]\sim\mathcal N(0,\sigma_0^2),\qquad\mathcal H_1:\ x[n]\sim\mathcal N(0,\sigma_1^2),\quad\sigma_1>\sigma_0.$$

Both centred at 0; \(\mathcal H_1\) is wider. Large \(|x|\) favours \(\mathcal H_1\).

$$p(\mathbf x\mid\mathcal H_i)=(2\pi\sigma_i^2)^{-N/2}\exp\Bigl(-\frac1{2\sigma_i^2}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}x[n]^2\Bigr).$$ $$L=\Bigl(\frac{\sigma_0}{\sigma_1}\Bigr)^N\exp\Bigl[\Bigl(\frac1{2\sigma_0^2}-\frac1{2\sigma_1^2}\Bigr)\sum_n x[n]^2\Bigr].$$

Log-LRT (official with-answers key; the Answers_oldexams file drops the squares — typesetting bug):

$$\ln L=N\ln\frac{\sigma_0}{\sigma_1}-\frac{\sum x[n]^2}{2\sigma_1^2}+\frac{\sum x[n]^2}{2\sigma_0^2}\;\gtrless\;\ln\gamma.$$

Because \(1/\sigma_0^2>1/\sigma_1^2\), the coefficient of \(\sum x^2\) is positive \(\Rightarrow\) decide \(\mathcal H_1\) when \(\sum x[n]^2\) is large (two-sided tails in 1-D). Energy detector, not a mean detector.

Single sample, \(L=1\): the PDFs cross at \(\pm x_{\mathrm{th}}\). Narrow tall peak \(=\mathcal H_0\), wide short peak \(=\mathcal H_1\). \(\mathcal X_1\) is outside the lines.

$$P_D=2\int_{x_{\mathrm{th}}}^\infty p(x\mid\mathcal H_1)\,dx,\qquad P_{\mathrm{FA}}=2\int_{x_{\mathrm{th}}}^\infty p(x\mid\mathcal H_0)\,dx.$$

Official numbers. \(\sigma_1=3\sigma_0\), \(x_{\mathrm{th}}=\sigma_1=3\sigma_0\). Given for \(x\sim\mathcal N(0,\sigma^2)\):

$$\Pr(0\le x\le\sigma)=0.341,\quad\Pr(\sigma\le x\le 2\sigma)=0.136,\quad\Pr(2\sigma\le x\le 3\sigma)=0.021.$$

Under \(\mathcal H_1\), \(x_{\mathrm{th}}=\sigma_1\) is one own-standard-deviation out:

$$P_D=2\bigl(0.5-0.341\bigr)=2\cdot 0.159=0.318.$$

Under \(\mathcal H_0\), \(x_{\mathrm{th}}=3\sigma_0\) is three own-sd out:

$$\Pr(0\le x\le 3\sigma_0)=0.341+0.136+0.021=0.498,\qquad P_{\mathrm{FA}}=2(0.5-0.498)=0.004.$$

Official: \(P_D=0.318\), \(P_{\mathrm{FA}}=0.004\).

3. Laplacian, different rates — sum of \(|x|\)

2022 4c, no official key. Algebra follows the printed exam (including the flipped \(\gtrless\)).

$$p(x\mid\mathcal H_0)=\tfrac12 e^{-|x|},\qquad p(x\mid\mathcal H_1)=e^{-2|x|}.$$

\(\mathcal H_1\) has larger rate \(\Rightarrow\) narrower Laplace. Large \(|x|\) favours \(\mathcal H_0\). One sample: \(L(x)=2e^{-|x|}\). IID:

$$L(\mathbf x)=2^N\exp\Bigl(-\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}|x_n|\Bigr).$$ $$2^N e^{-\sum|x_n|}\;\underset{\mathcal H_0}{\overset{\mathcal H_1}{\gtrless}}\;\gamma\qquad\Rightarrow\qquad -\sum|x_n|\;\underset{\mathcal H_0}{\overset{\mathcal H_1}{\gtrless}}\;\ln\gamma-N\ln 2.$$

Multiply by \(-1\) (flip the inequality):

$$\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}|x_n|\;\underset{\mathcal H_1}{\overset{\mathcal H_0}{\gtrless}}\;N\ln 2-\ln\gamma=:\gamma'.$$

Decide \(\mathcal H_0\) when the sum of absolute values is large. That is the printed 2022 4c inequality. Threshold \(\gamma'=\ln(2^N/\gamma)\).

For \(N=1\), \(Y=|X|\) is exponential: \(p(y\mid\mathcal H_0)=e^{-y}\), \(p(y\mid\mathcal H_1)=2e^{-2y}\), \(y>0\), and \(y\;\underset{\mathcal H_1}{\overset{\mathcal H_0}{\gtrless}}\;\gamma'\). At \(\gamma'=1\), decide \(\mathcal H_1\) for small \(y\) (left of 1).

  • \(P_{\mathrm{FA}}=P(y<1\mid\mathcal H_0)=\int_0^1 e^{-y}\,dy\): shade under the solid \(e^{-y}\) from 0 to 1.
  • \(P_M=P(y>1\mid\mathcal H_1)=\int_1^\infty 2e^{-2y}\,dy\): shade under the dashed \(2e^{-2y}\) from 1 to \(\infty\).

Do not shade the usual right tail. Numerical 2022 4e–g values (\(P_{\mathrm{FA}}=0.1\Rightarrow P_D=0.19\), Bayes \(y=\ln 3\)) are derived from the printed exam; there is no official key for those numbers. Do not treat them as published letters.

4. Exponential rates — threshold on \(x\)

Jan 2020 Q5, official key.

$$\mathcal H_0:\ p(x)=\lambda_0 e^{-\lambda_0 x},\qquad\mathcal H_1:\ p(x)=\lambda_1 e^{-\lambda_1 x},\qquad x\ge 0,\ \lambda_1>\lambda_0>0.$$

\(\mathcal H_1\) decays faster \(\Rightarrow\) more mass near 0. Large \(x\) favours \(\mathcal H_0\).

$$L(x)=\frac{\lambda_1}{\lambda_0}\exp\bigl[-(\lambda_1-\lambda_0)x\bigr].$$

\(L>\gamma\) iff \(x\) is smaller than a threshold (inequality flips). For \(\gamma=1\):

$$x_{\mathrm{th}}=\frac{\ln(\lambda_1/\lambda_0)}{\lambda_1-\lambda_0}.$$

Official numbers \(\lambda_1=2\), \(\lambda_0=0.1\): \(x_{\mathrm{th}}=\ln 20/1.9\approx 1.58\).

CDF of \(\mathrm{Exp}(\lambda)\): \(P(X\le x_{\mathrm{th}})=1-e^{-\lambda x_{\mathrm{th}}}\). Because we decide \(\mathcal H_1\) when \(x<x_{\mathrm{th}}\):

$$P_D=1-e^{-\lambda_1 x_{\mathrm{th}}},\qquad P_{\mathrm{FA}}=1-e^{-\lambda_0 x_{\mathrm{th}}}.$$

(Official writes \(\int_{-\infty}^{x_{\mathrm{th}}}\) but the exponential lives on \(x\ge 0\); the closed form is right.) Hint on the paper: design so \(P_D>P_{\mathrm{FA}}\) — that is the left-tail choice.

\(P_D\) and \(P_{\mathrm{FA}}\) with \(Q\), erf, and exponential CDFs

Gaussian, right-tail test \(x>\lambda'\), \(x\sim\mathcal N(\mu,\sigma^2)\):

$$P(x>\lambda')=Q\Bigl(\frac{\lambda'-\mu}{\sigma}\Bigr)=\frac12\Bigl[1-\operatorname{erf}\Bigl(\frac{\lambda'-\mu}{\sigma\sqrt 2}\Bigr)\Bigr].$$

Two-sided energy test \(|x|>x_{\mathrm{th}}\), zero-mean: double the one-sided tail.

Exponential / Laplace folded to \(y>0\), rate \(\lambda\), CDF \(1-e^{-\lambda y}\):

  • Decide \(\mathcal H_1\) on the right (\(y>\lambda'\)): \(P=e^{-\lambda\lambda'}\) (survival).
  • Decide \(\mathcal H_1\) on the left (\(y<\lambda'\)): \(P=1-e^{-\lambda\lambda'}\) (CDF).

Official erf table — Jan 2019 Q3 = SLT7.1

\(\mathcal H_0=\mathcal N(0,1)\), \(\mathcal H_1=\mathcal N(\mu_1,\sigma_1)\), threshold \(\lambda'\), right-tail.

$$P_{\mathrm{FA}}=\tfrac12\bigl[1-\operatorname{erf}(\lambda'/\sqrt 2)\bigr],\qquad P_D=\tfrac12\bigl[1-\operatorname{erf}((\lambda'-\mu_1)/(\sigma_1\sqrt 2))\bigr].$$

The table they give is \(\operatorname{erf}(Z/\sqrt 2)\) vs \(Z\), so you look up \(Z=(\lambda'-\mu)/\sigma\) directly. Values used in the key: \(Z=1\Rightarrow\operatorname{erf}=0.683\), \(Z=2\Rightarrow\operatorname{erf}=0.954\), and \(\operatorname{erf}\) is odd.

How, first row: \(\lambda'=1\), \(\mathcal H_0\): \(Z=1\), \(\operatorname{erf}=0.683\), \(P_{\mathrm{FA}}=\frac12(1-0.683)=0.1585\approx 0.159\). Row \(\lambda'=1,\mu_1=3,\sigma_1=1\): \(Z=(1-3)/1=-2\), \(\operatorname{erf}(-2)=-0.954\), \(P_D=\frac12(1-(-0.954))=0.977\). Last row \(P_{\mathrm{FA}}\): \(Z=2\), \(\frac12(1-0.954)=0.023\).

Worked: resit 24 Jan 2020 Q4 — Laplace vs \(\mathcal N(0,1)\)

Official key. \(p(y\mid\mathcal H_1)=\frac14 e^{-|y|/2}\) (Laplace), \(p(y\mid\mathcal H_0)=(2\pi)^{-1/2}e^{-y^2/2}\). Set \(L=1\):

$$\frac{\tfrac14 e^{-|y|/2}}{(2\pi)^{-1/2}e^{-y^2/2}}=1\Rightarrow -|y|+y^2=2\ln 4-\ln(2\pi)\Rightarrow y_{\mathrm{th}}=\pm 1.5884.$$

Decide \(\mathcal H_1\) (Laplace, heavier tails) for large \(|y|\). Shade: \(P_M\) is the centre of the Laplace between \(\pm y_{\mathrm{th}}\); \(P_{\mathrm{FA}}\) is the Gaussian tails outside \(\pm y_{\mathrm{th}}\).

$$P_D=\int_{1.5884}^\infty\tfrac12 e^{-y/2}\,dy=e^{-1.5884/2}=0.4519,$$ $$P_{\mathrm{FA}}=2Q(1.5884)=2\times 0.0559=0.1118.$$

(The integral uses evenness / the one-sided Laplace piece; equivalent to \(2\int_{y_{\mathrm{th}}}^\infty\frac14 e^{-y/2}\,dy\).) Official: \(y_{\mathrm{th}}=\pm 1.5884\), \(P_D=0.4519\), \(P_{\mathrm{FA}}=0.1118\).

Matched filter

Detect a known length-\(N\) waveform \(s_n\) in WGN:

$$\mathcal H_0:\ x_n=w_n,\qquad\mathcal H_1:\ x_n=s_n+w_n,\qquad w_n\sim\mathrm{IID}\ \mathcal N(0,\sigma^2).$$

Mean-shift with a vector mean \(s\), not a DC. LRT:

$$\ln L=\frac1{\sigma^2}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}x_n s_n-\frac1{2\sigma^2}\sum_n s_n^2\;\gtrless\;\ln\gamma\qquad\Rightarrow\qquad\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}x_n s_n\;\underset{\mathcal H_0}{\overset{\mathcal H_1}{\gtrless}}\;\sigma^2\ln\gamma+\tfrac12\sum_n s_n^2=\lambda'.$$

Left side = correlator (inner product \(\mathbf x^\top\mathbf s\)). Implement as an FIR filter, sample at the end:

$$y_n=\sum_{l=0}^{N-1}x_l h_{n-l},\qquad h_n=s_{N-1-n}\quad\text{(time-reversed }s\text{)}.$$

Then \(y_{N-1}=\sum_l x_l s_l\). Compare \(y_{N-1}\) to \(\lambda'\).

Why this maximises SNR. \(\mathrm{SNR}=\mathbb E^2[y_{N-1}\mid\mathcal H_1]/\mathrm{Var}[y_{N-1}\mid\mathcal H_0]=(\mathbf h^\top\mathbf s)^2/(\sigma^2\,\mathbf h^\top\mathbf h)\). Cauchy–Schwarz: \((\mathbf h^\top\mathbf s)^2\le(\mathbf h^\top\mathbf h)(\mathbf s^\top\mathbf s)\), equality iff \(\mathbf h=c\mathbf s\). Among all linear filters, the matched filter is SNR-optimal.

Resit 2020 / 2022 4a, official: “SNR at the output is maximised” is the true statement. \(h[n]=s[n]\) without time-reversal is false unless you correlate instead of convolve. The filter also exists for coloured noise after whitening; “only for uncorrelated noise” is not the definition.

Traps

  1. Which tail. \(\mathcal H_1\) wider / heavier-tailed \(\Rightarrow\) decide \(\mathcal H_1\) on large \(|x|\). \(\mathcal H_1\) narrower / faster exponential \(\Rightarrow\) decide \(\mathcal H_1\) on small \(x\). Flipping \(\ln\) of a decaying exponential flips \(\gtrless\).
  2. \(\mathcal H_0\) is just the label in the PDF. In Nov 2019 the energy detector has \(\mathcal H_1=\) larger variance.
  3. NP vs Bayes threshold. Do not mix \(\alpha\) with costs.
  4. \(P_{\mathrm{FA}}\) is always under \(\mathcal H_0\), over \(\mathcal X_1\). Not “the overlap”. Not under \(\mathcal H_1\).
  5. Log-LRT for Gaussians of unequal variance uses \(\sum x_n^2\), not \(\sum x_n\).
  6. Matched filter is \(h[n]=s[N-1-n]\), sampled at \(n=N-1\).
  7. Inverted LRT in some old keys. Write \(L=p_1/p_0\).

One sample, \(\mathcal H_0:\mathcal N(0,\sigma_0^2)\), \(\mathcal H_1:\mathcal N(0,\sigma_1^2)\) with \(\sigma_1>\sigma_0\). Thresholds at \(\pm x_{\mathrm{th}}\) where \(L=1\). Shade \(P_{\mathrm{FA}}\) and \(P_D\). What is the sufficient statistic for \(N\) samples?

Solution
